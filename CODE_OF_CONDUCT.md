# Code of Conduct

## Why this document exists

Glaneur is a small, personal, volunteer-maintained project. Anyone who opens an
issue, suggests an idea, reviews code, translates the interface or submits a
pull request is helping it, and deserves to be treated with respect.

This code of conduct describes how we expect people to behave in the project's
spaces, how to report a problem, and how problems are addressed. It is meant to
be short, readable in any language, and applied with good sense rather than
legalism.

## Our commitment

We want participation in Glaneur to be a welcoming and harassment-free
experience for everyone, whatever their age, body, disability (visible or not),
ethnicity, origin, nationality, caste, sex characteristics, gender identity or
expression, sexual orientation, family status, religion or belief, language,
education, social or economic status, appearance, or level of experience.

Many contributors do not write English as a first language. Clumsy wording is
not rudeness: read generously and ask before assuming bad intent.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be kind and patient.** Assume good faith, especially with newcomers and
  first-time contributors.
- **Be constructive.** Criticise ideas and code, never people. Explain *why*
  something should change, and suggest how.
- **Be precise and honest.** Share what you have actually observed (logs,
  versions, steps to reproduce) and say clearly when you are guessing.
- **Accept feedback gracefully**, and give it in a way you would like to
  receive it.
- **Own your mistakes.** Acknowledge them, apologise to those affected when it
  matters, and learn from them.
- **Respect people's time and limits.** The maintainer works on this project in
  their spare time; there is no guaranteed response delay. Contributors may
  also step away at any time.
- **Protect privacy.** Remove tokens, passwords, personal paths or personal
  data from logs and screenshots before posting them.
- **Respect the people behind the sites Glaneur talks to.** Glaneur uses public
  APIs politely on purpose. Requests aimed at bypassing access restrictions,
  overloading a server or ignoring a site's terms are out of scope and will be
  declined.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Insults, slurs, demeaning jokes or comments about a person or a group.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private, including repeated unwanted
  contact after being asked to stop.
- Threats of violence, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information (address, e-mail, real identity,
  private messages) without their explicit permission.
- Deliberate disruption: trolling, spam, flame wars, bad-faith arguments,
  derailing issues.
- Pressuring or shaming someone for not doing unpaid work, or for the speed at
  which they do it.
- Any other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a
  collaborative, professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in all of the project's spaces: the repository, issues
(including those opened through the application's built-in bug report), pull
requests, reviews, discussions, commit messages, release notes and translation
files. It also applies when someone represents the project elsewhere, and to
behaviour outside these spaces when it directly affects the safety of
participants.

Please include what happened, where (links are best), when, and who was
involved, if you know. You do not need to have every detail, and you do not
need to be the person directly affected.

Reports are handled confidentially. The reporter's identity is not shared
without their consent, and nobody will face retaliation for reporting in good
faith.

**If your concern is about the maintainer**, or you do not feel comfortable
contacting them, use GitHub's own
[report abuse](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
mechanism. As a single-maintainer project, Glaneur cannot offer an independent
review panel, and we prefer to say so openly.

## Addressing and repairing harm

The aim is to stop harm, protect the people affected and, where possible, allow
everyone to keep contributing. Responses are proportionate to the situation
and take into account intent, impact, and whether the behaviour is repeated.
The possible steps, from the lightest to the most serious, are:

1. **Clarification.** A private note explaining what was a problem and why.
   Often a misunderstanding, and often the end of it.
2. **Warning.** A clear request to change behaviour, possibly with a request
   to apologise or to edit or remove the content. Further incidents lead to
   the next steps.
3. **Temporary restriction.** Comments hidden or locked, and a temporary ban
   from interacting with the project. Once it ends, the person is welcome back.
4. **Permanent ban.** For serious incidents (harassment, threats, doxxing) or
   for a sustained pattern after earlier steps.

The maintainer may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and
other contributions that break this code, and will explain the decision when it
is safe and useful to do so.

Someone who has been asked to change their behaviour can repair things by
acknowledging the impact, apologising sincerely to those affected, not
repeating the behaviour and respecting any restriction. That is what makes a
return to the project possible.
